## What is Proof-of-Control?

**Open Verification** is open, independent, graded verification that a system's controls are
implemented and held — method and evidence both open to inspection, graded by how much you must
trust to believe it. Verification is open when what you must trust is a mechanism anyone can
verify rather than a party.

**Proof-of-Control** is open verification for AI agents: for each control it claims, across six
domains, a conformant implementation MUST produce evidence — generated by the enforcing mechanism
at execution time — that the control held; place that evidence on the Verifiability Tiers; and
disclose its residual trust assumptions. A system has Proof-of-Control when, and only when, its
evidence reaches Tier 3 or Tier 4.

In plain terms: when your organization lets an AI agent act on its behalf — approve something,
move money, pull a record — Proof-of-Control is how you *prove*, rather than promise, that the
agent stayed inside the rules you set. Think of it like a public notary for your agents' actions,
with one difference that changes everything: no one has to trust the notary.

## The Six Domains of Verification

Each domain enumerates verifiable facts — deterministic, true-or-false statements about an
agent's execution ([Section 4](0.1/en/0x10-S04-What-Must-Be-Verified.md)):

1. **Provenance** — which model ran, its lineage, artifact and supply-chain origin
2. **Privacy** — what data was read and written
3. **Portability** — boundary crossings (organizational, jurisdictional, compute)
4. **Authorization** — authority granted, decisions within or against it, delegation validity
5. **Identity** — which agent and which principal ran
6. **Security** — integrity of the execution environment; that controls held; tools invoked

## The Verifiability Tiers

Evidence is graded by how much you must trust to believe it
([Section 6](0.1/en/0x10-S06-Evidence-and-Grading.md)):

| Tier | Name | Who you must trust | Proof-of-Control? |
| :---: | --- | --- | :---: |
| 1 | Assertion | The operator | No |
| 2 | Attestation | A third party, or the root-keeper | No |
| 3 | Independently Verifiable | The cryptographic mechanism | **Yes** |
| 4 | Self-Enforcing | The network protocol or continuous mathematical constraints | **Yes** |

The **binary threshold** falls between Tier 2 and Tier 3: below it, authenticated documentation;
above it, evidence. A system has Proof-of-Control when, and only when, its evidence reaches
Tier 3 or Tier 4.

## Who Made This

**[Advanced AI Society](https://advancedaisociety.org/)** convenes the field and stewards the
Proof-of-Control standard as a public good. It is a 501(c)(6) industry association led by
co-chairs **Ken Huang** and **Tricia Wang**. On completion, ownership transfers to the
Verifiable AI Foundation, the Society's affiliated 501(c)(3). The Proof-of-Control Lab is being
established as a community lab at Linux Foundation Decentralized Trust.

## Versioning

The standard is versioned; each release is a numbered folder, mirroring the approach used by
OWASP AISVS/ASVS. Version 1.0 is targeted for **February 1, 2027**.

├── 0.1/        <- Working Draft v0.1.4 (public comment)
